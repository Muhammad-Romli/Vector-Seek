# Animals Knowledge Base

## 1. Introduction

Animals are multicellular organisms in the kingdom Animalia. They obtain energy by consuming other organisms or organic material and are found in nearly every environment on Earth.


---

# 2. Animals

## 1. Lion

Lions are large social cats native mainly to African savannas and grasslands. They live in prides and cooperate to hunt prey such as zebras, wildebeest, and antelope.

## 2. Tiger

Tigers are solitary big cats found across forests, grasslands, and wetlands in Asia. They are powerful carnivores that hunt deer, wild pigs, and other mammals.

## 3. ElephantSSS

Elephants are the largest living land animals. They are herbivores that eat grasses, leaves, bark, roots, and fruit, while also modifying habitats by knocking down trees and creating paths.

## 4. Giraffe

Giraffes are extremely tall African herbivores adapted to browsing leaves from trees. Their long necks and tongues help them reach vegetation unavailable to many other grazers.

## 5. Zebra

Zebras are striped African relatives of horses. They primarily graze on grasses and live in herds that provide protection from predators such as lions and hyenas.

## 6. Wolf

Wolves are social carnivores belonging to the dog family. They hunt cooperatively in packs and commonly prey on deer, elk, and smaller mammals.

## 7. Brown Bear

Brown bears are large omnivorous mammals found in forests, mountains, tundra, and coastal regions. They eat berries, fish, insects, roots, mammals, and carrion.

## 8. Polar Bear

Polar bears are Arctic marine mammals specialized for cold environments and sea ice. They primarily hunt seals and depend heavily on marine ecosystems.

## 9. Giant Panda

Giant pandas are bears native to China's mountain forests. Their diet consists mainly of bamboo, despite their evolutionary relationship with carnivorous mammals.

## 10. Gorilla

Gorillas are large African apes that live in forest environments. They are mainly herbivorous and eat leaves, stems, shoots, and fruit.

## 11. Chimpanzee

Chimpanzees are highly intelligent African apes with complex societies. They eat fruit, leaves, insects, eggs, and occasionally small animals and are known for using tools.

## 12. Orangutan

Orangutans are tree-dwelling apes found in Southeast Asian rainforests. They mainly eat fruit but also consume leaves, bark, flowers, and insects.

## 13. Human

Humans are highly intelligent primates distributed across almost every terrestrial biome. Humans are omnivores and have profoundly altered ecosystems through agriculture, technology, urbanization, and industry.

## 14. Horse

Horses are domesticated and wild members of the equine family. They are grazing herbivores with adaptations for running across open grasslands and plains.

## 15. Cow

Cattle are large domesticated herbivores commonly raised for food, milk, leather, and other agricultural purposes. Their specialized digestive systems allow them to process fibrous grasses.

## 16. Dog

Dogs are domesticated mammals descended from ancient wolf populations. Their diets vary widely, and selective breeding has produced enormous variation in size, appearance, behavior, and working abilities.

## 17. Cat

Domestic cats are small carnivorous mammals descended from wildcat ancestors. They retain hunting behaviors such as stalking, pouncing, and chasing small prey.

## 18. Rabbit

Rabbits are small herbivorous mammals found in forests, grasslands, deserts, and human environments. They eat grasses, leaves, vegetables, and other plant material.

## 19. Kangaroo

Kangaroos are Australian marsupials adapted to grasslands, woodlands, and dry environments. They use powerful hind legs for hopping and primarily consume grasses and vegetation.

## 20. Koala

Koalas are Australian marsupials that live mainly in eucalyptus forests. They feed heavily on eucalyptus leaves, which provide limited energy and contain defensive chemicals.

## 21. Dolphin

Dolphins are intelligent marine mammals that often live in social groups called pods. They use echolocation to locate prey and primarily eat fish, squid, and other marine animals.

## 22. Blue Whale

Blue whales are the largest known animals in Earth's history. They are filter feeders that consume enormous quantities of krill using baleen plates.

## 23. Humpback Whale

Humpback whales are large baleen whales known for long migrations and complex vocalizations. They feed mainly on krill and small schooling fish.

## 24. Great White Shark

Great white sharks are large marine predators found in coastal and offshore waters. They eat fish, rays, seals, sea lions, and other marine animals.

## 25. Whale Shark

Whale sharks are the largest living fish. Despite their size, they are gentle filter feeders that consume plankton, small fish, and other tiny organisms.

## 26. Octopus

Octopuses are intelligent marine mollusks with eight arms and highly developed nervous systems. They are carnivores that eat crabs, fish, mollusks, and other small animals.

## 27. Squid

Squid are fast-moving marine mollusks with tentacles and sophisticated eyes. They eat fish, crustaceans, and other marine organisms and are important prey for whales and larger fish.

## 28. Penguin

Penguins are flightless birds adapted for swimming. They eat fish, squid, and krill and use their modified wings as underwater flippers.

## 29. Eagle

Eagles are large birds of prey with powerful talons and excellent eyesight. They hunt fish, mammals, reptiles, and other birds depending on the species.

## 30. Owl

Owls are nocturnal or crepuscular birds of prey with excellent hearing and specialized silent flight. Their prey includes rodents, insects, reptiles, amphibians, and birds.

## 31. Hawk

Hawks are predatory birds with sharp talons and hooked beaks. They commonly hunt rodents, reptiles, birds, insects, and other small animals.

## 32. Falcon

Falcons are fast-flying birds of prey known for aerial hunting. The peregrine falcon is particularly famous for its extremely high diving speed.

## 33. Parrot

Parrots are intelligent birds recognized for curved beaks, strong feet, and colorful plumage. Most eat seeds, nuts, fruits, flowers, and other plant material.

## 34. Crow

Crows are highly intelligent birds capable of problem-solving and tool use. They are omnivores that eat insects, seeds, fruit, carrion, eggs, and human-associated food.

## 35. Flamingo

Flamingos are long-legged aquatic birds that often live in shallow lakes and wetlands. They filter-feed on algae, small crustaceans, and other tiny organisms.

## 36. Ostrich

Ostriches are the largest living birds and cannot fly. They inhabit African savannas and deserts and primarily eat plants, seeds, and other available foods.

## 37. Crocodile

Crocodiles are large aquatic reptiles with powerful jaws and armored bodies. They are ambush predators that eat fish, birds, mammals, reptiles, and other animals.

## 38. Alligator

Alligators are large freshwater reptiles found primarily in the United States and China. They eat fish, turtles, birds, mammals, and other prey.

## 39. Komodo Dragon

Komodo dragons are the largest living lizards and are native to several Indonesian islands. They are carnivores that hunt or scavenge deer, pigs, and other animals.

## 40. Cobra

Cobras are venomous snakes found mainly in Africa and Asia. They use venom to subdue prey and may eat rodents, birds, frogs, lizards, and other animals.

## 41. Python

Pythons are nonvenomous constricting snakes. They capture prey using their jaws and muscular bodies and can consume mammals, birds, reptiles, and other animals.

## 42. Sea Turtle

Sea turtles are marine reptiles that spend most of their lives in oceans. Their diets vary by species and can include seagrass, algae, jellyfish, sponges, and crustaceans.

## 43. Frog

Frogs are amphibians associated with moist habitats and freshwater breeding environments. Adult frogs commonly eat insects and other small animals.

## 44. Salamander

Salamanders are amphibians with elongated bodies and tails. Many live in moist forests or freshwater environments and eat insects, worms, crustaceans, and other small organisms.

## 45. Butterfly

Butterflies are insects with a distinctive four-stage life cycle: egg, larva, pupa, and adult. Caterpillars usually eat plants, while adults commonly drink flower nectar.

## 46. Moth

Moths are close relatives of butterflies and occupy a huge variety of habitats. Caterpillars commonly consume plant material, while adult moth diets vary among species.

## 47. Honey Bee

Honey bees are social insects that live in colonies. Workers collect nectar and pollen, while their movement between flowers makes them important pollinators.

## 48. Ant

Ants are highly social insects that live in organized colonies. They can be predators, scavengers, herbivores, or omnivores depending on the species.

## 49. Beetle

Beetles are one of the most diverse groups of animals, with enormous variation in size, habitat, and diet. Species may consume plants, fungi, insects, carrion, or decaying material.

## 50. Dragonfly

Dragonflies are flying insects associated with freshwater environments during their larval stages. Adults are aerial predators that capture mosquitoes, flies, and other insects.

## 51. Spider

Spiders are arachnids rather than insects. Most are predators that capture prey using webs, ambush tactics, or active hunting and commonly eat insects.

## 52. Scorpion

Scorpions are venomous arachnids found in deserts, forests, grasslands, and other habitats. They use pincers and venomous stingers to capture prey and defend themselves.

## 53. Crab

Crabs are crustaceans found in marine, freshwater, and terrestrial environments. Depending on the species, they can be scavengers, predators, herbivores, or omnivores.

## 54. Lobster

Lobsters are marine crustaceans with hard exoskeletons and powerful claws. They commonly eat fish, mollusks, worms, algae, and carrion.

## 55. Shrimp

Shrimp are small crustaceans found in oceans and freshwater. Their diets vary and may include algae, plankton, detritus, microorganisms, and small animals.

## 56. Starfish

Starfish, more accurately called sea stars, are marine echinoderms. Many species are predators or scavengers that eat mollusks, small animals, and organic material.

## 57. Jellyfish

Jellyfish are gelatinous marine animals with tentacles containing specialized stinging cells. They capture plankton, fish larvae, and other small organisms.

## 58. Sea Anemone

Sea anemones are marine cnidarians related to jellyfish and corals. They use stinging tentacles to capture fish, crustaceans, and other prey.

## 59. Coral

Corals are colonies of tiny cnidarian animals called polyps. Reef-building corals form complex habitats that support thousands of other marine species.

## 60. Earthworm

Earthworms are segmented soil-dwelling invertebrates that consume organic material and soil particles. Their burrowing improves soil structure, aeration, and nutrient cycling.

## 61. Snail

Snails are mollusks found in marine, freshwater, and terrestrial environments. Many species are herbivores that scrape algae or vegetation with a specialized feeding structure called a radula.

## 62. Slug

Slugs are shell-less terrestrial mollusks. Many feed on leaves, fungi, algae, and decaying vegetation and can be important decomposers.

## 63. Octopus

Octopuses are soft-bodied cephalopods capable of camouflage, problem-solving, and complex behavior. They are active marine predators.

## 64. Beaver

Beavers are semi-aquatic rodents famous for constructing dams. They eat bark, aquatic plants, leaves, and other vegetation while creating wetlands used by many species.

## 65. Otter

Otters are semi-aquatic mammals found in rivers, lakes, wetlands, and coastal waters. They commonly eat fish, crustaceans, amphibians, and other aquatic prey.

## 66. Bat

Bats are the only mammals capable of sustained powered flight. Depending on the species, they may eat insects, fruit, nectar, fish, or small animals.

## 67. Sloth

Sloths are slow-moving mammals that spend much of their lives in trees. They mainly eat leaves and have slow metabolisms suited to their low-energy diet.

## 68. Meerkat

Meerkats are small social mammals living in southern African deserts and grasslands. They eat insects, spiders, scorpions, reptiles, and other small animals.

## 69. Hyena

Hyenas are African and Asian carnivorous mammals with powerful jaws. They hunt prey and also consume carrion, making them both predators and scavengers.

## 70. Cheetah

Cheetahs are specialized African predators known for exceptional running speed. They primarily hunt small to medium-sized hoofed animals such as gazelles.

## 71. Leopard

Leopards are adaptable big cats found in forests, grasslands, mountains, and semi-arid regions. They hunt mammals, birds, reptiles, and other animals and often drag kills into trees.

## 72. Moose

Moose are the largest members of the deer family. They inhabit northern forests and wetlands and feed on leaves, aquatic plants, twigs, and other vegetation.

## 73. Deer

Deer are herbivorous hoofed mammals found across many habitats. Their diets include grass, leaves, shoots, fruit, bark, and other vegetation.

## 74. Penguin

Penguins are flightless seabirds adapted to aquatic environments. Their dense feathers, streamlined bodies, and fat layers help them survive cold marine conditions.

## 75. Platypus

The platypus is a semi-aquatic mammal native to Australia. It has a bill, webbed feet, and electroreception and feeds mainly on aquatic invertebrates.

## 76. Chicken
Chickens are domesticated birds raised worldwide for eggs and meat. They are omnivores that eat seeds, grains, insects, vegetation, and small invertebrates.

## 77. Pig
Pigs are intelligent domesticated mammals found in farms and, through related wild species, many natural habitats. They are omnivores that eat roots, fruits, plants, insects, and small animals.

## 78. Goat
Goats are domesticated hoofed mammals adapted to farms, mountains, grasslands, and dry environments. They are herbivores that browse leaves, shrubs, grasses, and other vegetation.

## 79. Sheep
Sheep are domesticated ruminant mammals commonly raised for wool, meat, and milk. They are herbivores that primarily graze on grasses and other plants.

## 80. Donkey
Donkeys are domesticated members of the horse family that originated from African wild asses. They are herbivores adapted to dry environments and commonly eat grasses, weeds, and other vegetation.

## 81. Duck
Ducks are waterfowl found in wetlands, rivers, lakes, and coastal environments. Depending on the species, they eat aquatic plants, seeds, insects, crustaceans, and small fish.

## 82. Goose
Geese are large waterfowl commonly associated with wetlands, lakes, grasslands, and agricultural areas. Many species are primarily herbivorous, feeding on grasses, aquatic vegetation, and seeds.

## 83. Turkey
Turkeys are large ground-dwelling birds native to North America, with domesticated varieties raised worldwide. They are omnivores that eat seeds, fruits, insects, plants, and small animals.

## 84. Pigeon
Pigeons are adaptable birds found naturally in cities, cliffs, forests, and agricultural environments. They mainly eat seeds and grains but can consume a wide variety of human-associated foods.

## 85. Sparrow
Sparrows are small birds commonly found around grasslands, farms, gardens, and cities. They primarily eat seeds and grains while also feeding insects to their young.

## 86. Swan
Swans are large waterfowl known for their long necks and strong swimming abilities. They mainly consume aquatic plants, grasses, algae, and occasionally small aquatic organisms.

## 87. Goldfish
Goldfish are freshwater fish commonly kept as pets and ornamental animals. They are omnivores that can consume algae, aquatic plants, insects, larvae, and small organisms.

## 88. Salmon
Salmon are fish known for migrations between freshwater rivers and the ocean. Depending on their life stage, they consume insects, plankton, crustaceans, and smaller fish.

## 89. Tuna
Tuna are fast-swimming marine fish found in tropical and temperate oceans. They are carnivorous predators that eat smaller fish, squid, and crustaceans.

## 90. Clownfish
Clownfish are small brightly colored marine fish commonly associated with sea anemones. They eat algae, plankton, small crustaceans, and food scraps around their host anemones.

## 91. Seahorse
Seahorses are unusual fish with upright bodies and curled tails. They use their tube-like snouts to suck up tiny crustaceans and other planktonic organisms.

## 92. Angelfish
Angelfish refers to several groups of attractive freshwater and marine fish. Their diets vary from algae and plants to small invertebrates and plankton.

## 93. Goldfish
Goldfish are domesticated freshwater fish derived from wild carp. They are commonly kept in aquariums and ponds and have flexible omnivorous diets.

## 94. Guppy
Guppies are small freshwater fish popular in aquariums. They eat algae, plant matter, insect larvae, tiny crustaceans, and other small organisms.

## 95. Salmon
Salmon are migratory fish that can travel from oceans into freshwater rivers to reproduce. They occupy different food-web positions throughout their life cycle.

## 96. Mackerel
Mackerel are schooling marine fish found in temperate and tropical oceans. They are active predators that feed on plankton, small fish, and crustaceans.

## 97. Goldfish
Goldfish are hardy freshwater fish that have been selectively bred into many colors and body shapes. They feed on a mixture of plant and animal material.

## 98. Rat
Rats are highly adaptable rodents found across cities, farms, forests, and many other environments. They are omnivores that consume grains, fruits, insects, meat, and discarded food.

## 99. Mouse
Mice are small rodents found in forests, grasslands, agricultural areas, and human buildings. They commonly eat seeds, grains, fruits, insects, and other available foods.

## 100. Squirrel
Squirrels are rodents found in forests, parks, gardens, and urban environments. Many species collect and eat nuts, seeds, fruits, fungi, and plant material.

## 101. Hamster
Hamsters are small rodents known for their expandable cheek pouches. They primarily eat seeds, grains, fruits, vegetation, and occasional insects.

## 102. Guinea Pig
Guinea pigs are domesticated rodents native to South America. They are herbivores that primarily consume grasses, hay, vegetables, and other plant material.

## 103. Hedgehog
Hedgehogs are small mammals covered with defensive spines. They are mainly insectivorous but can also eat worms, amphibians, small vertebrates, fruits, and other foods.

## 104. Fox
Foxes are adaptable members of the dog family found in forests, grasslands, deserts, mountains, and cities. They are omnivores that eat rodents, birds, insects, fruit, and carrion.

## 105. Raccoon
Raccoons are highly adaptable mammals native to North America and increasingly found in urban environments. They are omnivores that eat fruit, insects, fish, eggs, small animals, and human food.

## 106. Skunk
Skunks are mammals famous for their strong defensive spray. They are omnivores that eat insects, worms, small animals, fruits, roots, and other foods.

## 107. Squirrel
Squirrels are agile rodents that often climb trees and store food for later use. Their diets commonly include nuts, seeds, fruits, fungi, and vegetation.

## 108. Badger
Badgers are sturdy mammals belonging to several related groups. Depending on the species, they eat insects, worms, rodents, roots, fruit, and other small animals.

## 109. Camel
Camels are large mammals adapted to deserts and other dry environments. They are herbivores capable of eating grasses, leaves, thorny plants, and vegetation with little available water.

## 110. Llama
Llamas are domesticated South American camelids adapted to highland environments. They are herbivores that graze on grasses and other vegetation.

## 111. Alpaca
Alpacas are domesticated camelids closely related to llamas. They are herbivores that feed primarily on grasses and are especially associated with high-altitude South American regions.

## 112. Rhinoceros
Rhinoceroses are large herbivorous mammals found mainly in Africa and Asia. Depending on the species, they graze on grasses or browse on leaves, branches, and shrubs.

## 113. Hippopotamus
Hippopotamuses are massive semi-aquatic African mammals that spend much of the day in rivers and lakes. They mainly feed on grasses and can travel onto land at night to graze.

## 114. Gorilla
Gorillas are powerful social apes native to African forests. They mainly eat leaves, stems, shoots, fruit, and other plant material.

## 115. Lemur
Lemurs are primates found naturally only on Madagascar. Their diets vary by species and can include fruit, leaves, flowers, insects, and small animals.

## 116. Baboon
Baboons are large African and Arabian primates that live in forests, savannas, and rocky environments. They are omnivores that consume fruit, seeds, roots, insects, and small animals.

## 117. Macaque
Macaques are adaptable monkeys found across Asia and parts of North Africa. They are generally omnivorous and eat fruit, leaves, seeds, insects, and small animals.

## 118. Chinchilla
Chinchillas are small rodents native to the Andes Mountains of South America. They are herbivores that feed on grasses, seeds, roots, and other vegetation.

## 119. Armadillo
Armadillos are armored mammals native primarily to the Americas. Many species eat insects and other invertebrates, while some also consume plants, fruit, and small animals.

## 120. Anteater
Anteaters are specialized mammals from Central and South America that feed primarily on ants and termites. Their long snouts and sticky tongues are adapted for extracting insects from nests.

## 121. Pangolin
Pangolins are scaly mammals found in Africa and Asia. They specialize in eating ants and termites and use long, sticky tongues to capture their prey.

## 122. Aardvark
Aardvarks are nocturnal African mammals specialized for eating ants and termites. Their strong claws allow them to break into insect colonies, while their long tongues collect prey.

## 123. Wombat
Wombats are burrowing Australian marsupials with powerful bodies and short legs. They are herbivores that feed mainly on grasses, roots, and other vegetation.

## 124. Tasmanian Devil
Tasmanian devils are carnivorous marsupials native to Tasmania. They are powerful scavengers that consume carrion, small animals, birds, and other available food.

## 125. Kiwi
Kiwis are small flightless birds native to New Zealand. They use their long bills to probe soil for worms, insects, larvae, and other small invertebrates.
